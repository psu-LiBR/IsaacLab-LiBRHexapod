Switch penalty · 5×–100×

25 paired policies, replays and measurements across six algorithms. DQN and DDQN retain near-one-body-length forward motion with low command-switch counts; PPO has shorter dwell intervals. SAC-D 1-step trades displacement against lateral drift. SAC-D 5-step is presented as low-displacement controls.

Filter: every leg has at most 4 high-level command changes per complete 1-second cycle; zero observed falls and torso contact. This is not a six-leg sum. Straightness and dwell are reported separately; no universal hardware acceptance threshold is claimed.

Original only: scale the existing switch penalty. Original + added switch: keep that scaled penalty and add the per-leg binary-command-flip cost. Original reference: −0.005; added cost reference: 0.0004. Tested multipliers: 5×, 10×, 30×, 100×. Actual values appear on every card.

6 seconds of simulation represent 6 nominal cycles; switching statistics use the 5 complete phase-aligned cycles per environment (64 environments). Forward body lengths per cycle use the full 6-second measurement duration and a 0.315 m body length (friction 0.21). A video is one illustrative environment; metrics aggregate the evaluation batch. Zero command switches do not mean a leg is physically motionless.

Within each algorithm: fewer maximum switches, then less lateral drift, then more forward displacement. These are complementary candidates, not a claim that the first is universally best. Original models and ONNX exports agree on 2,049 synthetic inputs per model; this is a software check, not a robot trial.
